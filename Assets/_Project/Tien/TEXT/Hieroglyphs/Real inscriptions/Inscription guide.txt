Figure 1
A relief from the tomb of Seti I in the Valley of Kings.
Translation: Goddess, Nekhebt, the White One of Nekhen, given health, life and dominion. The living Good God, Son of Amen, born of Mut, Lady of Asheru in the palace of Ipet Esut. The Osiris King, Lord of the Two Lands, Son of Re, of his body, his beloved, Lord of Appearences, True Voice before Osiris, Foremost of the West.

Figure 2
A record of an offering for the decorations of Karnak Temple by Amenhotep III.
Translation: 4820 deben of turquoise and 6823 deben of carnelian.

Figure 3
Anubis, God Guardian of the Necropolis, takes the souls of Egypt's nine enemies to the underworld.

Figure 4
Twin cartouches containing the name of King Tutankhamun.

Figure 5
Twin cartouches of Ramses II, containing his official and personal names. The first is the origin of Ozymandias in Shelley's poem of the same name.

Figure 6
From Theban tomb TT48, belonging to Surer ("Chief Steward, At the head of the King, Overseer of the Cattle of Amun"). It depicts three of the 'nine bows' (the nine traditional enemies of Egypt): Lybia, Nubia and Asia, their hands tied behind their backs in submission.

Figure 7
The titles and names of Amenemhet whose nickname was Surer, from his tomb in Thebes.
Translation: The Hereditary Prince and Nomarch, greatest of the Favoured, Acquaintance of the Lord of the Two Lands, King's Scribe, he who is at the head of the king, Amen-em-het, say of him Surer, justified.

Figure 8
The five great names of Senusert I, from the way-station at Karnak.
Translation: Horus, Nesu-bit, beloved of Amun-Re, Chief over the Two Lands, living forever. Nebty, Ankh-mesu the Golden Horus, Ankh-Mesu the son of Re, given all life, stability and dominion and all health living forever.

Figure 9
The daughter of King Teti who became the wife of the Vizier Mereruka. As well as having a royal name she also had a beautiful name.
Translation: King's daughter, of his body, Priestess of Hathor, Lady of the Sycamore, Har-watet-khet, her beautiful name is Sesh-seshet.

Figure 10
Part of a text from a funerary stela describing the son of the tomb owners.
Translation: Their beloved son, who causes their names to live, the King's Acquaintance, his true and beloved.

Figure 11
Cartouche of Cleopatra, her name is spelled phonetically.

Figure 12
Cartouche of Ptolemy, his name is also spelled phonetically.

Source: Understanding Hieroglyphs by Hilary Wilson (I highly recommend this book!)
